\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}

Because tests are generated from the existing policy structure and table column names, the LLM may not anticipate every failure mode or generate tests for it. As a result, some problems may never be exposed or corrected.
Additionally, current evaluations cover only low-dimensional tasks following the MDP assumption. A natural next step is to scale the method to more complex tasks with larger feature spaces or possibly a POMDP formulation \cite{kaelbling1998planning}.
The method may also benefit from a fine-tuned LLM and/or a more carefully designed, higher-level domain-specific language such as Scenic \cite{fremont2019scenic}, which may be easier to update than PyTorch.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\label{sec:takeaway}

We introduced iterative policy structure refinement through semantic rollout analysis.
The method alternates demonstration-based parameter fitting with LLM-generated diagnostics and structural edits, linking execution and revision through semantically meaningful latents.
With one demonstration per task, refinement improves imitation learning, generalizes to unseen task configurations, and reduces subsequent reinforcement learning resets.
