\section{Semantic analysis of policies and diagnostics}
\label{app:extended}

We analyze the strategies represented by the policies and the coverage of generated diagnostics to understand how rollout analysis guides structural refinement.

\subsection{Policy strategy coverage}
\label{app:strategy-coverage}

The policy structure makes both the controller's strategies and the targets of its diagnostics explicit.
We manually group computations in the Car Racing programs into speed-control, steering-control, and special-case strategies (Table~\ref{tab:semantic}).
The initial structure contains ten categories; refinement retains all ten and adds steering toward the outside of curves and braking during sharp turns.
MLES contains fifteen categories, including more rules for grass, curbs, off-track recovery, and stopping.
Our structured policies instead concentrate on general relationships between task state and actions, with specific nuanced behavior handled by continuous parameters.
These categories summarize broad control strategies rather than every operator or structural edit.

\subsection{Diagnostic categories and coverage}
\label{app:coverage}

The generated analyses connect these strategies to measurable properties of execution through four diagnostic categories.
Together they examine the values a policy computes, whether those values follow its definitions, how they relate to behavior, and how control signals evolve over time.

\paragraph{Summary statistics.}
Means, standard deviations, ranges, and signs characterize observations, semantic latents, and trainable parameters.
For example, the slip coefficient's sign tests whether its contribution to target speed agrees with the intended speed-reducing strategy.

\paragraph{Reconstruction errors.}
Analyses recompute a latent or action mean from its declared inputs and compare the result with the rollout value.
These checks test arithmetic consistency and help distinguish a mismatched computation from variation introduced by action sampling or logging.
In the slip example, accurate target-speed reconstruction establishes that the program executes the specified formula even while its learned coefficient violates the expected sign.

\paragraph{Correlations and regressions.}
Pearson correlations and linear regressions measure relationships between observations, latents, and actions across the trajectory.
For example, steering as a function of lateral offset tests whether the car tends to recenter itself, connecting a named strategy to its expression during execution.

\paragraph{Temporal behavior and action limits.}
Other diagnostics examine abrupt changes in control signals and the frequency of action saturation.
These checks identify behavior that may be obscured by aggregate return or trajectory-wide averages.

Table~\ref{tab:test-coverage} summarizes generated diagnostics across three refinement rounds, with 13, 10, and 10 test functions, respectively.
Their coverage changes as the policy evolves: later rounds add checks of angular velocity, off-track behavior, and steering at sharp turns.
This combination turns the semantic structure into an interface for testing and revision: each diagnostic identifies a policy quantity, a behavioral expectation, and a computation that can be edited.
Appendix~\ref{app:records} shows how these connections guide updates to features, parameter constraints, and state-dependent interactions.

\section{Policy refinement examples}
\label{app:records}

The following examples illustrate how generated diagnostics guide policy changes in Car Racing.

\subsection{Off-track recovery behavior}
\label{app:recovery-record}

The recovery behavior analysis filters rollout steps where the absolute lateral offset exceeds \texttt{half\_track\_width}, then checks whether steering points toward the track center under the recorded sign convention.
In the 1,000-step rollout, 74 steps are off-track and only 14.9\% of these have center-directed steering.
This identifies a state-dependent failure that aggregate return does not locate: the centering contribution is present, but rarely determines the steering direction when recovery is needed.
The revision adds an off-track indicator and a trainable nonnegative recovery strength, forming a multiplier that increases centering beyond the track boundary (Figure~\ref{fig:latent-traces}).
The other steering contributions remain in place.

\section{Implementation details}
\label{app:training}

\subsection{Refinement procedure and execution}
\label{app:procedure}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:refinement} summarizes the data flow and permitted interaction branches.
Here $\Sigma(C)$ denotes the table-column schema exposed by the environment and the policy's logging interface.
Analysis-code generation depends on this schema and the policy program, independently of the numerical rollout values.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Refinement through semantic rollout analysis}
\label{alg:refinement}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Task specification $S$, demonstrations $\mathcal D$, environment
\STATE $C\gets\mathrm{LLM}(S,\text{generation specification})$
\WHILE{the refinement procedure continues}
    \STATE $\theta\gets\mathrm{Fit}(C,\mathcal D)$; $\hat\pi\gets\pi_{C,\theta}$
    \STATE $T\gets\mathrm{LLM}(S,C,\Sigma(C),\text{diagnostic prompt})$
    \STATE $X\gets\mathrm{RolloutLog}(C,\theta)$
    \REPEAT
        \STATE $r\gets\mathrm{Execute}(T,X)$
        \STATE $b\gets\mathrm{LLM}(S,C,T,r,\text{revision context})$
        \IF{$b$ requests additional diagnostics}
            \STATE $T\gets$ additional analysis code
        \ENDIF
    \UNTIL{$b$ proposes a revised program or stops refinement}
    \IF{$b$ stops refinement} \STATE \textbf{break} \ENDIF
    \STATE $C\gets$ revised program from $b$
\ENDWHILE
\STATE \textbf{return} $\hat\pi$; optionally apply post-refinement RL
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The refinement loop has a configurable iteration budget, with a default maximum of six rounds and early termination through the LLM's stop response.
Additional tests run on the current table before the next revision decision.
If fitting a revised program fails, the LLM can regenerate it using the preceding policy and diagnostic context, with up to five attempts.


\subsection{Post-refinement reinforcement learning}
\label{app:rl}

We optimize a clipped policy-gradient objective using group-relative reward-to-go and window likelihoods.
For trajectory $i$, let $T_i$ be its length and let $\mathcal I_t$ contain trajectories valid at time $t$.
We compute
\begin{equation}
 R_t^i=\sum_{u=t}^{T_i-1}\gamma^{u-t}r_u^i,
 \qquad A_t^i=\frac{R_t^i-\mu_t}{\sigma_t+10^{-8}},
 \label{eq:rl-advantage}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_t$ and $\sigma_t$ are the population mean and standard deviation over $\mathcal I_t$.

The likelihood ratio multiplies action likelihoods over a window of $L$ valid steps.
In log-probability form,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{it}(\theta)=\exp\!\left[
 \sum_{u=t}^{t+L-1}\bigl(\log\pi_\theta(a_u^i\mid s_u^i)
 -\log\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_u^i\mid s_u^i)\bigr)\right].
 \label{eq:rl-ratio}
\end{equation}
For valid window starts, the clipped objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal J(\theta)=\mathbb E_{i,t}\!\left[
 \min\!\left(\rho_{it}(\theta)A_t^i,
 \operatorname{clip}(\rho_{it}(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_t^i\right)\right].
 \label{eq:rl-objective}
\end{equation}
An entropy bonus encourages exploration, with its coefficient decaying linearly over training.
The optimizer defaults are $\gamma=0.98$, clipping threshold $\epsilon=0.2$, Adam learning rate $0.007$, 20 sampled episodes per update, three optimization epochs, and initial entropy coefficient 0.01; the default Car Racing likelihood window is 150 steps.
Unlike GSPO's length-normalized sequence ratio~\citep{zheng2025group}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:rl-ratio} uses the product of likelihoods within each window.
Trajectories share an initial condition and are grouped by time step for advantage estimation.

\section{Diagnostic and revision prompts}
\label{app:prompts}

The diagnostic and revision prompts connect policy semantics to numerical execution evidence.
The LLM first proposes analyses using the code and schema, then receives their results as the basis for a possible edit.

\subsection{Hypothesis generation and analysis execution}
\label{app:diagnostic-prompts}

The system prompt asks the LLM to consider nominal behavior and possible anomalies before writing analysis code.
It requests explicit hypotheses, defensive functions over a dataframe, and output in a common results file.
The full numerical rollout is an input to these generated functions rather than part of the initial diagnostic-generation context.
\VerbatimInput[breaklines=true,breakanywhere=true,fontsize=\footnotesize]{appendices/prompts/diagnostics_system.txt}

The per-policy template supplies the environment description, model definition, and available columns.
\VerbatimInput[breaklines=true,breakanywhere=true,fontsize=\footnotesize]{appendices/prompts/diagnostics_input.txt}

\subsection{Revision, additional tests, and termination}
\label{app:revision-prompts}

The revision prompt offers three responses: provide an updated program, request more diagnostic tests, or indicate that no further action is needed.
It allows changes to the computations and parameterization while preserving the model interface and logging outputs.
